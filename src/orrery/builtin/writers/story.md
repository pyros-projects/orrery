You write one shot for the MiniMax H3 video model, which makes video with sound. Picture 1 is the first frame of the clip and Picture 2 its last frame. The clip is one continuous take of {seconds} seconds: no cut, no dissolve, no jump in time.

{world}

How the shot is written:

SHOT {seconds}s: <camera move>
Two or three sentences in the present tense: what moves, in the order it happens, from how Picture 1 looks to how Picture 2 looks.
SFX: a sound; another sound

- Camera moves: push in, pull out, zoom in, zoom out, pan left, pan right, truck left, truck right, tilt up, tilt down, pedestal up, pedestal down, arc, tracking, static. Choose the move that turns the framing of Picture 1 into the framing of Picture 2: closer in Picture 2 is a push in, farther is a pull out, the same framing is static.
- Name what is in the pictures by what it is (a red-haired boy in a raincoat, a clay robot with a blue antenna), never as "Picture 1".
- Everything that differs between the pictures changes on screen, through a visible action, in one take: a dog asleep on the sofa that ends at the front door gets up and trots over to it. Never "the scene cuts to", "then we see" or "the scene shifts".
- The shot starts exactly as Picture 1 and ends exactly as Picture 2.
- SFX: the sounds you hear, each next to its cause, separated by semicolons; always at least one sound. Speech, if anyone talks, goes on its own line: `NAME (how it sounds): the line.`

Never write wildcards, variables, choices, comments, headers, markdown or explanations.

An example, of other pictures:

SHOT 5s: push in, slow
A woman by the window sets down her cup, rises from the chair and walks across the room to the open door, where she stops with one hand on the frame.
SFX: a cup clinks on a saucer; footsteps on wooden boards

Look closely at both pictures, then write the shot. Start with "SHOT {seconds}s:". Answer with the shot alone.
